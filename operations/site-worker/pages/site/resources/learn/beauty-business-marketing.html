How to get more clients as a beauty professional

A practical visibility strategy for nail techs, lash artists, hairstylists and estheticians: get found, build trust and make the next step easy.

The client journey begins before someone books

“How do I get more nail clients?” or “How do I attract clients to my lash business?” are understandable questions. But more posting is not automatically more bookings. A new customer first has to discover your business, understand what you offer, see evidence you can deliver it and know what to do next.

That sequence matters for home-based professionals, suite renters, established salon staff and independent beauty brands. Start by auditing the steps a stranger would actually take.

Step 1: Make the business easy to understand

Look at your Instagram bio, TikTok profile, website and booking page. Can a new visitor answer these questions within seconds?

  • What services, education or resources do you offer?
  • Who is the offer for and what result does it help achieve?
  • What location or service area applies, if you see clients in person?
  • How much does the service cost or where can they see current prices?
  • What is the single next step: book, inquire, shop or learn?

If you no longer provide nail appointments under the business, remove obsolete booking messages. A professional profile should reflect the business customers can actually purchase from today.

Step 2: Create content from customer questions

Instead of guessing what to post, keep a running list of the questions clients already ask. A nail tech might answer “How long do Gel-X extensions last?”, “Why do detailed nail designs cost more?” or “How should I prepare for my appointment?” A lash artist might explain fill maintenance, aftercare or appointment expectations.

For each real question, make one short video, one carousel, one story and one saved FAQ. Let the explanation demonstrate your process, not only the finished work. Avoid implying outcomes you cannot substantiate.

Step 3: Give each piece of content a next step

One post might invite people to a booking page, while another invites them to download a guide. Don't send someone who wants a specific price to a generic homepage with no service details. Keep the destination aligned with the question or offer.

Step 4: Build trust with evidence

Use genuine examples of your work, transparent policies, service descriptions, clearly identified reviews and appropriate behind-the-scenes education. Never invent testimonials. Confirm permissions before sharing identifiable client photos or messages.

Step 5: Track what happens after discovery

Record basic metrics that inform decisions: number of inquiries, qualified inquiries, completed bookings or purchases, and the content that led visitors to your site. Views are useful, but a full calendar or successful order is a different measure.

What about Google and AI search?

Search engines can discover pages with clear titles, useful answers and crawlable links. For service-area businesses, accurate business details matter. For online education brands, question-led tutorials and consistent expertise are a better fit than chasing “nail salon near me” rankings when appointments are not offered.

Google’s Search Essentials recommends helpful, people-first content, descriptive words and crawlable links. There is no guaranteed first-page placement.

A seven-day starter challenge

  1. Day 1: audit your public business descriptions and links.
  2. Day 2: list ten actual customer questions.
  3. Day 3: film one clear answer using your own expertise.
  4. Day 4: make the booking or inquiry destination easy to find.
  5. Day 5: publish a second answer that demonstrates a process.
  6. Day 6: review questions, objections and inquiries.
  7. Day 7: repeat the piece of content that gave someone a useful next step.
